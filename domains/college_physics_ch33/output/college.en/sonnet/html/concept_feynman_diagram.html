🍃 Feynman Diagram

🍃 Feynman Diagram

Definition. A Feynman diagram is a pictorial representation of the mathematical terms in the perturbation expansion of the scattering matrix (S-matrix) in quantum field theory. Each diagram encodes a specific sequence of particle interactions, with lines representing particle propagators and vertices representing interaction events. More precisely, each distinct topology corresponds to a term in the perturbative series for the transition amplitude $\mathcal{M}$, from which observable quantities such as cross-sections and decay rates are derived. In quantum electrodynamics (QED), a vertex connects two fermion lines (solid) to one photon line (wavy), and carries a factor of the coupling constant $-ie\gamma^\mu$, where $e$ is the elementary charge and $\gamma^\mu$ are the Dirac matrices.

Worked Example. Consider electron–electron scattering ($e^-e^- \to e^-e^-$) at lowest order. There are two contributing diagrams at order $e^2$: the $t$-channel and $u$-channel exchanges. In the $t$-channel diagram, the two incoming electrons exchange a virtual photon. The amplitude for this process is

$$ \mathcal{M}_t = \frac{(-ie)^2}{t}\,\bar{u}(p_3)\gamma^\mu u(p_1)\,\eta_{\mu\nu}\,\bar{u}(p_4)\gamma^\nu u(p_2), $$

where $t = (p_1 - p_3)^2$ is the Mandelstam variable and $\eta_{\mu\nu}$ is the photon propagator in Feynman gauge. The full amplitude is $\mathcal{M} = \mathcal{M}_t - \mathcal{M}_u$ (with a relative sign from fermion exchange). The differential cross-section follows from $|{\mathcal{M}}|^2$ via the standard phase-space formula.

Key Theorem. The Feynman rules guarantee that every diagram contributing to a process at order $\alpha^n$ (where $\alpha = e^2/4\pi\epsilon_0\hbar c \approx 1/137$) is suppressed by $\alpha^n$ relative to the tree-level result. Because $\alpha \operatorname{ll} 1$, the perturbative expansion converges rapidly in QED, and leading-order diagrams dominate. This is why QED predictions agree with experiment to extraordinary precision — for example, the anomalous magnetic moment of the electron matches theory at ten significant figures.

Lab Exercise. Draw all distinct Feynman diagrams for Compton scattering ($\gamma e^- \to \gamma e^-$) at lowest order in $\alpha$. For each diagram, identify the internal propagator, label all four-momenta, and write down the corresponding Feynman amplitude $\mathcal{M}$ using the QED Feynman rules. Verify that the sum of both diagrams is gauge invariant by confirming that replacing the external photon polarization $\epsilon^\mu$ with its four-momentum $k^\mu$ yields $\mathcal{M} = 0$.
