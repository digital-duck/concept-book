🍃 Four Basic Forces

🍃 Four Basic Forces

Definition. All interactions in nature reduce to four fundamental forces (interactions): the gravitational force, the electromagnetic force, the weak nuclear force, and the strong nuclear force. These forces differ dramatically in strength, range, and the particles that mediate them.

Force Relative Strength Range Mediating Boson
Strong $1$ $\sim 10^{-15}$ m Gluons
Electromagnetic $10^{-2}$ $\infty$ Photons ($\gamma$)
Weak $10^{-13}$ $\sim 10^{-18}$ m $W^{\pm}, Z^0$
Gravitational $10^{-38}$ $\infty$ Gravitons (theoretical)

Gravitational and electromagnetic forces obey inverse-square laws ($F \propto r^{-2}$) and have infinite range. The nuclear forces are short-range: the strong force binds quarks into hadrons and nucleons into nuclei over distances $r \lesssim 10^{-15}$ m, while the weak force governs radioactive $\beta$-decay over $r \lesssim 10^{-18}$ m.

Worked Example. Compare the gravitational and electrostatic forces between two protons separated by $r = 1.00 \times 10^{-15}$ m (roughly one femtometer, the nuclear scale).

Electrostatic repulsion:

$$ F_E = k_e \frac{e^2}{r^2} = (8.99 \times 10^9)\frac{(1.60 \times 10^{-19})^2}{(1.00 \times 10^{-15})^2} \approx 230 \text{ N} $$

Gravitational attraction:

$$ F_G = G\frac{m_p^2}{r^2} = (6.67 \times 10^{-11})\frac{(1.67 \times 10^{-27})^2}{(1.00 \times 10^{-15})^2} \approx 1.86 \times 10^{-34} \text{ N} $$

The ratio $F_E / F_G \approx 10^{36}$, confirming gravity is negligible at nuclear scales. Yet protons remain bound in nuclei because the strong force overwhelms electromagnetic repulsion at $r < 10^{-15}$ m.

Key Theorem (Unification). The electromagnetic and weak forces unify into a single electroweak force at energies $E \gtrsim 100$ GeV, described by the Glashow–Salam–Weinberg model. This was experimentally confirmed by the discovery of the $W^{\pm}$ and $Z^0$ bosons at CERN in 1983, earning the Nobel Prize in Physics. Grand Unified Theories (GUTs) attempt to incorporate the strong force at energies $\sim 10^{15}$ GeV, and a complete Theory of Everything would include gravity.

Lab Exercise. Using a Millikan-type apparatus, measure the charge-to-mass ratio $e/m_e$ for an electron. From your result, compute $F_E/F_G$ between two electrons at separation $r = 1.00$ nm and compare with the tabulated relative strengths above. Discuss which force dominates in atomic versus nuclear length scales, and identify which of the four forces is responsible for the contact force you experience when pressing your hand against a table.
