🌱 Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle

🌱 Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle

Definition. The Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle establishes a fundamental lower bound on the precision with which certain pairs of conjugate observables can be simultaneously determined. For position $x$ and momentum $p_x$, the principle states:

$$ \Delta x \, \Delta p_x \geq \frac{\hbar}{2} $$

where $\Delta x$ is the standard deviation of position measurements, $\Delta p_x$ is the standard deviation of momentum measurements, and $\hbar = h/(2\pi) \approx 1.055 \times 10^{-34}$ J·s is the reduced Planck constant. An analogous relation holds for energy and time:

$$ \Delta E \, \Delta t \geq \frac{\hbar}{2} $$

This is not a statement about measurement imprecision due to experimental limitations; it reflects an intrinsic feature of quantum mechanical wave functions. A state with a well-defined position is a spatially localized wave packet, which requires a broad superposition of momentum eigenstates, and vice versa.

Worked Example. An electron is confined within an atomic nucleus of diameter $d \approx 1.0 \times 10^{-14}$ m. Estimate the minimum uncertainty in the electron's momentum and the corresponding kinetic energy.

Taking $\Delta x \approx d$, the minimum momentum uncertainty is:

$$ \Delta p_x \geq \frac{\hbar}{2\Delta x} = \frac{1.055 \times 10^{-34}}{2(1.0 \times 10^{-14})} \approx 5.3 \times 10^{-21} \text{ kg·m/s} $$

The kinetic energy associated with this momentum (using $K = p^2/2m_e$ with $m_e = 9.11 \times 10^{-31}$ kg) is:

$$ K \approx \frac{(\Delta p_x)^2}{2m_e} \approx \frac{(5.3 \times 10^{-21})^2}{2(9.11 \times 10^{-31})} \approx 1.5 \times 10^{-11} \text{ J} \approx 96 \text{ MeV} $$

Since electrons emitted from nuclear beta decay carry only a few MeV, this result demonstrates that electrons cannot be permanently confined within the nucleus — a foundational argument supporting the neutrino hypothesis.

Key Theorem. The uncertainty principle follows rigorously from the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality applied to the inner product space of square-integrable wave functions. For any two non-commuting operators $\hat{A}$ and $\hat{B}$ with commutator $[\hat{A}, \hat{B}] = i\hat{C}$, the generalized relation is $\Delta A \, \Delta B \geq |\langle \hat{C} \rangle|/2$.

Lab Exercise. Using a single-slit diffraction apparatus, pass a laser beam through a slit of adjustable width $a$. Record the angular half-width $\theta$ of the central diffraction maximum. Show that the transverse momentum spread $\Delta p_y \approx p\sin\theta$ satisfies $\Delta y \, \Delta p_y \approx h$, where $\Delta y \approx a$. Compare measured values to the theoretical minimum $\hbar/2$ and discuss sources of the numerical discrepancy.
