🍃 Quark Color

🍃 Quark Color

Definition. In quantum chromodynamics (QCD), color charge is the fundamental conserved quantity that governs the strong nuclear force, analogous to electric charge in electromagnetism. Unlike electric charge, which takes only positive and negative values, color charge comes in three varieties conventionally labeled red ($r$), green ($g$), and blue ($b$), together with their anticolor counterparts $\bar{r}$, $\bar{g}$, and $\bar{b}$. The label "color" is purely metaphorical — quarks carry no actual chromatic property — but the analogy is precise: just as white light combines all visible colors, physically observable (color-neutral) hadrons are formed by color combinations that sum to colorless, or white, states. This requirement is called color confinement: isolated colored particles are never observed in nature.

Quarks are assigned a single color charge; antiquarks carry the corresponding anticolor. Gluons, the mediators of the strong force, carry one color and one anticolor simultaneously (e.g., $r\bar{b}$), making them self-interacting — a key distinction from photons.

Worked Example. Consider the proton, a baryon composed of two up quarks and one down quark ($uud$). For the proton to be colorless, its three quarks must collectively carry one unit each of red, green, and blue:

$$ |p\rangle \propto \epsilon^{ijk}\, u_i\, u_j\, d_k, $$

where $\epsilon^{ijk}$ is the fully antisymmetric Levi-Civita symbol and $i,j,k \in \{r,g,b\}$. The antisymmetric combination $rgb - rbg + gbr - grb + brg - bgr$ ensures the total color state is a singlet under the $\mathrm{SU}(3)_c$ gauge group — mathematically equivalent to mixing equal intensities of red, green, and blue.

Key Theorem (Color Singlet Confinement). QCD predicts that only color-singlet (colorless) combinations of quarks and gluons can propagate as free asymptotic states. Baryons achieve this via the antisymmetric $rgb$ combination; mesons achieve it via a quark–antiquark pair $q\bar{q}$ in a $r\bar{r} + g\bar{g} + b\bar{b}$ superposition.

Lab Exercise. Deep inelastic scattering experiments measure the ratio $R = \sigma(e^+e^- \to \text{hadrons})/\sigma(e^+e^- \to \mu^+\mu^-)$. Using the prediction

$$ R = N_c \sum_f Q_f^2, $$

where $N_c = 3$ is the number of colors and $Q_f$ is the electric charge of quark flavor $f$, calculate $R$ for center-of-mass energies just above the $b\bar{b}$ threshold (include $u$, $d$, $s$, $c$, $b$ quarks) and compare with the measured value $R \approx 3.56$ to confirm the experimental evidence for three color charges.
