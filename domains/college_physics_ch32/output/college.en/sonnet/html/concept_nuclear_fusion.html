🍃 Nuclear Fusion

🍃 Nuclear Fusion

Definition. Nuclear fusion is the process by which two light atomic nuclei combine to form a heavier nucleus, releasing energy when the product nucleus has a greater binding energy per nucleon than the reactants. The energy released per reaction follows directly from the mass defect $\Delta m$ via Einstein's mass-energy equivalence:

$$ Q = \Delta m \, c^2 = \bigl(m_1 + m_2 - m_{\text{product}}\bigr)c^2 $$

For fusion to occur, the reacting nuclei must overcome the Coulomb potential barrier—the electrostatic repulsion between their positive charges. This requires kinetic energies on the order of $10\text{–}100\,\text{keV}$, achievable only at temperatures exceeding $10^7\,\text{K}$ (thermonuclear conditions) or through quantum tunneling at somewhat lower temperatures.

Worked Example. Consider the deuterium–tritium (D–T) reaction, the basis of most fusion reactor designs:

$$ {}^2_1\text{H} + {}^3_1\text{H} \longrightarrow {}^4_2\text{He} + {}^1_0\text{n} $$

Using tabulated atomic masses: $m({}^2\text{H}) = 2.014102\,\text{u}$, $m({}^3\text{H}) = 3.016049\,\text{u}$, $m({}^4\text{He}) = 4.002602\,\text{u}$, $m_n = 1.008665\,\text{u}$.

$$ \Delta m = (2.014102 + 3.016049) - (4.002602 + 1.008665) = 0.018884\,\text{u} $$

Converting: $Q = 0.018884 \times 931.5\,\text{MeV/u} \approx 17.6\,\text{MeV}$.

This single reaction releases roughly 3.5 times the energy of the most energetic fission reaction per unit mass of fuel, illustrating fusion's extraordinary energy density.

Key Theorem — Lawson Criterion. For a fusion plasma to achieve net energy gain (ignition), the product of plasma ion number density $n$ and energy confinement time $\tau_E$ must satisfy:

$$ n\tau_E \gtrsim 10^{20}\,\text{s\,m}^{-3} $$

at a temperature $T \approx 10^8\,\text{K}$ for the D–T reaction. This criterion encodes the competition between thermonuclear power output (scaling as $n^2$) and energy loss mechanisms, and defines the engineering target for devices such as tokamaks and inertial confinement facilities.

Lab Exercise. Using the atomic masses provided, calculate the $Q$-value for the proton–proton chain's first step, $p + p \rightarrow {}^2_1\text{H} + e^+ + \nu_e$, and determine what fraction of the initial rest-mass energy is released. Then estimate the minimum temperature required to classically surmount the Coulomb barrier between two protons separated by $r \approx 1\,\text{fm}$, using $U = ke^2/r$ and comparing to $k_BT$.
