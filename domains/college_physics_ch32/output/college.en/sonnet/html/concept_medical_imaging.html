🌸 Medical Imaging

🌸 Medical Imaging

Definition. Medical imaging encompasses a collection of physics-based techniques that produce spatial maps of internal anatomical structures or physiological processes without invasive surgery. The primary modalities — X-ray radiography, computed tomography (CT), magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), positron emission tomography (PET), and ultrasound — each exploit a distinct physical interaction between a probe (electromagnetic radiation, radiofrequency fields, or mechanical waves) and biological tissue. Image contrast arises from spatially varying material properties such as electron density, nuclear spin relaxation time, or acoustic impedance.

In X-ray radiography, transmitted intensity follows Beer–Lambert attenuation:

$$ I = I_0 \, e^{-\mu x} $$

where $I_0$ is the incident photon fluence, $\mu$ is the linear attenuation coefficient of the tissue ($\text{m}^{-1}$), and $x$ is the path length through the material. Bone ($\mu \approx 100\ \text{m}^{-1}$ at 60 keV) attenuates far more strongly than soft tissue ($\mu \approx 20\ \text{m}^{-1}$), producing the characteristic bright skeletal regions on a film or digital detector.

Worked Example. A diagnostic chest X-ray uses 80 keV photons. A beam passes through 3.0 cm of lung tissue ($\mu = 18\ \text{m}^{-1}$) and then 0.50 cm of rib bone ($\mu = 95\ \text{m}^{-1}$). Find the fraction of photons transmitted.

$$ \frac{I}{I_0} = e^{-\mu_{\text{lung}} x_{\text{lung}}} \cdot e^{-\mu_{\text{bone}} x_{\text{bone}}} $$ $$ = e^{-(18)(0.030)} \cdot e^{-(95)(0.0050)} = e^{-0.54} \cdot e^{-0.475} \approx 0.583 \times 0.622 \approx 0.36 $$

Approximately 36% of photons reach the detector behind the rib, while 63% reach the detector behind pure lung tissue, generating measurable contrast between the two regions.

Key Theorem (Radon Inversion). CT reconstruction rests on the Radon transform: a full set of line-integral projections $p(\phi, s)$ acquired at angles $\phi$ uniquely determines the 2-D attenuation map $\mu(x,y)$ via filtered back-projection. The reconstruction filter in frequency space applies a ramp kernel $|k|$ before back-projecting each projection, restoring spatial resolution lost by simple summation.

Lab Exercise. Using a benchtop X-ray source and a digital flat-panel detector, image a phantom containing cylinders of water, acrylic, and aluminum. Record transmitted counts for each material at two tube voltages (60 kV and 100 kV). Calculate $\mu$ for each material at each voltage, compare with tabulated values, and explain the energy dependence in terms of photoelectric absorption ($\propto Z^3/E^3$) versus Compton scattering (weakly energy dependent).
