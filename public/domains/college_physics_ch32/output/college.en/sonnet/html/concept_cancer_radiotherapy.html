🌸 Cancer Radiotherapy

🌸 Cancer Radiotherapy

Definition. Cancer radiotherapy is the clinical application of ionizing radiation to destroy malignant cells by depositing sufficient energy to damage DNA beyond the cell's capacity for repair. The key physical quantity is the absorbed dose, $D$, defined as the energy deposited per unit mass of tissue:

$$ D = \frac{\Delta E}{\Delta m}, \quad [D] = \text{Gy} \equiv \text{J kg}^{-1}. $$

Because biological damage depends on radiation type, the dose equivalent $H$ weights the absorbed dose by the radiation weighting factor $w_R$:

$$ H = w_R \, D, \quad [H] = \text{Sv (sievert)}. $$

For photons and electrons $w_R = 1$; for protons $w_R \approx 2$; for alpha particles $w_R = 20$. The therapeutic ratio quantifies treatment efficacy as the ratio of tumor-cell kill to normal-tissue damage — maximizing this ratio is the central engineering challenge of radiotherapy.

Worked Example. A tumor of mass $m = 25\text{ g}$ is irradiated with a $^{60}$Co gamma source delivering $\Delta E = 2.5 \times 10^{-2}\text{ J}$ to the tumor volume. Find the absorbed dose and dose equivalent.

$$ D = \frac{\Delta E}{m} = \frac{2.5 \times 10^{-2}\text{ J}}{25 \times 10^{-3}\text{ kg}} = 1.0\text{ Gy}. $$

Since $^{60}$Co emits gamma rays ($w_R = 1$):

$$ H = w_R \, D = (1)(1.0\text{ Gy}) = 1.0\text{ Sv}. $$

A curative course typically delivers $60$–$70\text{ Gy}$ in fractions of $\approx 2\text{ Gy}$ per session, exploiting the differential repair capacity between healthy and tumor tissue.

Key Theorem (Bragg Peak Principle). Heavy charged particles (protons, carbon ions) lose energy according to the Bethe–Bloch relation, depositing a sharply localized maximum — the Bragg peak — at depth $R \propto E_0^{1.77}/\rho$, where $E_0$ is the initial kinetic energy and $\rho$ is tissue density. Tuning $E_0$ positions the peak precisely at tumor depth, sparing overlying healthy tissue and delivering negligible dose beyond the target.

Lab Exercise. Using published depth–dose tables for a $150\text{ MeV}$ proton beam in water ($\rho = 1.00\text{ g cm}^{-3}$), determine the beam energy required to shift the Bragg peak from $16.0\text{ cm}$ to $20.0\text{ cm}$ depth. Calculate the percentage increase in $E_0$ and discuss how a spread-out Bragg peak (SOBP) is constructed by superimposing beams of different energies to uniformly irradiate a tumor of finite longitudinal extent.
