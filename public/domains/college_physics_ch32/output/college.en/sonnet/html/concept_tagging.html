🍃 Tagging

🍃 Tagging

Definition. Radioactive tagging (also called radioactive labeling or tracing) is the technique of incorporating a radioisotope into a molecule or compound so that the substance can be tracked in situ by detecting its emitted radiation. The tagged atom is chemically identical to its stable counterpart, so it participates in biological, chemical, or physical processes without perturbation, while its radioactive decay provides a continuous, detectable signal. The fundamental requirement is that the radioisotope's half-life $T_{1/2}$ be long enough to complete the experiment yet short enough to minimize radiation dose to the subject. The activity $A$ of a tagged sample follows

$$ A(t) = A_0 \, e^{-\lambda t}, \qquad \lambda = \frac{\ln 2}{T_{1/2}}, $$

where $A_0$ is the initial activity and $\lambda$ is the decay constant.

Worked Example. A physician administers $^{99\text{m}}\text{Tc}$ (technetium-99m, $T_{1/2} = 6.01\,\text{h}$) tagged to a phosphonate compound for a bone scan. The injected activity is $A_0 = 740\,\text{MBq}$. Find the activity remaining after 24 h.

$$ \lambda = \frac{\ln 2}{6.01\,\text{h}} = 0.1153\,\text{h}^{-1} $$ $$ A(24\,\text{h}) = 740\,\text{MBq} \times e^{-(0.1153)(24)} = 740 \times e^{-2.767} \approx 740 \times 0.0630 \approx 46.6\,\text{MBq} $$

After four half-lives the activity has fallen to roughly $\frac{1}{16}$ of its initial value, demonstrating that $^{99\text{m}}\text{Tc}$ clears rapidly while still providing adequate signal during the imaging window.

Key Theorem. The minimum detectable quantity of a tagged substance is governed by the specific activity $S_A = \lambda N_A / M$, where $N_A$ is Avogadro's number and $M$ is the molar mass. Because $\lambda$ can be large even for nanogram quantities, radioactive tagging can detect concentrations far below those accessible by conventional chemical analysis — a sensitivity advantage of many orders of magnitude over stable tracers.

Lab Exercise. A sample of $^{131}\text{I}$ ($T_{1/2} = 8.02\,\text{days}$) with initial activity $3.70 \times 10^5\,\text{Bq}$ is used to study thyroid iodine uptake. (a) Calculate the decay constant $\lambda$ in units of $\text{s}^{-1}$. (b) Determine the activity after 16 days. (c) If the detector requires a minimum activity of $5.00 \times 10^3\,\text{Bq}$, find the maximum elapsed time $t_{\max}$ for which the experiment remains feasible.
