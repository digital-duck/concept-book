🍃 Radiopharmaceutical

🍃 Radiopharmaceutical

Definition. A radiopharmaceutical is a compound in which one or more atoms are radioactive nuclides, designed so that the molecule selectively localizes in a specific tissue or organ following administration to a patient. The radiopharmaceutical serves a dual purpose: the pharmaceutical component governs biodistribution (where the compound accumulates), while the radionuclide component emits ionizing radiation that either enables diagnostic imaging or delivers a therapeutic radiation dose to the target site. Formally, if $N(t)$ is the number of radionuclide atoms present at time $t$, the activity is

$$ A(t) = \lambda N(t) = A_0 e^{-\lambda t}, $$

where $\lambda = \ln 2 / T_{1/2}$ is the decay constant and $T_{1/2}$ is the physical half-life. In vivo, the effective elimination of a radiopharmaceutical combines physical decay and biological clearance, yielding an effective half-life

$$ T_{\text{eff}} = \frac{T_{1/2}\, T_b}{T_{1/2} + T_b}, $$

where $T_b$ is the biological half-life.

Worked Example. Technetium-99m ($^{99\text{m}}$Tc) is the most widely used diagnostic radiopharmaceutical, with $T_{1/2} = 6.01\ \text{h}$. Suppose a patient is administered $A_0 = 740\ \text{MBq}$ of $^{99\text{m}}$Tc-labeled sestamibi for a cardiac perfusion scan, and the biological half-life in myocardial tissue is $T_b = 12.0\ \text{h}$. The effective half-life is

$$ T_{\text{eff}} = \frac{6.01 \times 12.0}{6.01 + 12.0} \approx 4.01\ \text{h}. $$

After $t = 8.0\ \text{h}$, the remaining activity in tissue is

$$ A(8.0) = 740\, e^{-(\ln 2 / 4.01)(8.0)} \approx 740 \times 0.251 \approx 186\ \text{MBq}. $$

Key Theorem (ALARA Principle and Dosimetry). The absorbed dose $D$ delivered by a radiopharmaceutical to a target organ of mass $m$ is

$$ D = \frac{\tilde{A}\, \Delta}{m}, $$

where $\tilde{A} = \int_0^\infty A(t)\, dt = A_0 / \lambda_{\text{eff}}$ is the cumulated activity and $\Delta$ is the mean energy emitted per nuclear transition. Minimizing $T_{\text{eff}}$ reduces $\tilde{A}$ and therefore reduces $D$, consistent with the ALARA (As Low As Reasonably Achievable) principle.

Lab Exercise. Iodine-131 ($T_{1/2} = 8.02\ \text{d}$) is administered as sodium iodide to treat hyperthyroidism. Given a biological half-life of $T_b = 80\ \text{d}$ in the thyroid and an initial activity of $A_0 = 370\ \text{MBq}$, calculate (a) $T_{\text{eff}}$, (b) the cumulated activity $\tilde{A}$, and (c) the activity remaining after 16 days. Discuss why $^{131}$I is preferable to a longer-lived iodine isotope for therapeutic applications.
