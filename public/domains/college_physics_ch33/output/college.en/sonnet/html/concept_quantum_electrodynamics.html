🍃 Quantum Electrodynamics

🍃 Quantum Electrodynamics

Definition. Quantum electrodynamics (QED) is the relativistic quantum field theory describing the interaction between electrically charged particles and the electromagnetic field. It quantizes both the matter field (electrons and positrons, governed by the Dirac equation) and the radiation field (photons), treating electromagnetic interactions as the exchange of virtual photons. The QED Lagrangian density is

$$ \mathcal{L} = \bar{\psi}(i\gamma^\mu D_\mu - m)\psi - \frac{1}{4}F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}, $$

where $\psi$ is the Dirac spinor field, $D_\mu = \partial_\mu + ieA_\mu$ is the gauge-covariant derivative, $A_\mu$ is the electromagnetic four-potential, and $F_{\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu A_\nu - \partial_\nu A_\mu$ is the electromagnetic field tensor. The coupling constant $e$ (the elementary charge) determines interaction strength, characterized by the dimensionless fine-structure constant $\alpha = e^2/(4\pi\epsilon_0 \hbar c) \approx 1/137$.

Worked Example: Electron–Positron Annihilation. Consider an electron ($e^-$) and positron ($e^+$) annihilating at rest to produce two photons: $e^- + e^+ \to 2\gamma$. Conservation of four-momentum requires both photons to emerge back-to-back, each carrying energy $E_\gamma = m_e c^2 = 0.511\ \text{MeV}$. The leading-order QED amplitude involves a single Feynman diagram with a virtual electron propagating between two photon vertices. The cross section for this process at threshold is

$$ \sigma = \frac{\pi r_e^2}{\beta}\left(1 - \beta^2\right)\left[\ln\frac{1+\beta}{1-\beta} - \beta\right], $$

where $r_e = e^2/(4\pi\epsilon_0 m_e c^2) \approx 2.82 \times 10^{-15}\ \text{m}$ is the classical electron radius and $\beta = v/c$ of the positron in the lab frame.

Key Theorem: Renormalizability. QED is perturbatively renormalizable: ultraviolet divergences appearing in loop integrals can be systematically absorbed into redefinitions of the electron mass and charge. This allows predictions to arbitrary order in $\alpha$. The anomalous magnetic moment of the electron, $a_e = (g-2)/2$, computed to tenth order in $\alpha$, agrees with experiment to better than one part in $10^{12}$, making QED the most precisely tested theory in physics.

Lab Exercise. Using a sodium iodide scintillation detector, measure the energy spectrum of photons from a $^{22}\text{Na}$ positron source. Identify the 511 keV annihilation peak and the Compton edge. Calculate the ratio of peak energy to electron rest mass energy and compare with the QED prediction $E_\gamma = m_e c^2$. Estimate detector energy resolution from the full width at half maximum of the annihilation peak.
